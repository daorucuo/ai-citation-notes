RegulationRegulation moves from content categories to generation methods

· What happened Two sources that do not syndicate each other reported it the same day: China's National Radio and Television Administration added AI-generated low-quality micro-dramas to the target list of a special enforcement campaign (Lianhe Zaobao 11-07 ／ Solidot 11-07). The same week, one AI micro-drama passed 12 billion views.

· What it means For the past two years the objects of regulation were platforms and content categories. This time it is how the content was generated. Enforcement tightening and a hundred-billion-view hit landing in the same week says the volume of this track got large enough that it had to be named.

· So what to watch The compliance boundary moves from "what the content looks like" to "where the content came from." The checkpoint therefore moves into production: who generated it, and with what, becomes a question you get asked.

Technical supplyChange one requirement, models split by 23 points

· What happened A public evaluation took 89 existing tasks and changed only the instructions, or only the starting code (median reference patch grew by just 17 lines, 3.5%). The spread between the highest and lowest of 15 configurations widened from 20.9 to 48.3 percentage points; six configurations that sat within 1.5 points of each other near 74% fanned out across 23.0 points. DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 74.2%→55.6%; Gemini 3.8 Flash 73.8%→53.4%.

· What it means A score is not a stability measure. It is one result, on this paper, under this set of requirements. And acceptance decisions lean on scores. One caveat must be stated: this does not mean leaderboards are worthless — the same study repaired the benchmark's reference bugs and re-graded the original submissions; the average moved only from 65.8% to 64.5%, 1.3 points. The benchmark was never that bad. What is bad is treating a score as reliability.

· So what to watch Wherever "we tested it" is the basis for a signature, the sheet that was tested was the original paper. The question is not "is it accurate" but "under what conditions is it accurate."

Source: MaxxBench research page 2026-11-07　〔has numbers〕〔primary·English〕

Business modelThe 56% gain belongs to a variety show

· What happened After China's first AIGC long-form drama to air on satellite TV, shares of Mango Excellent Media rose 56.19% in five trading sessions. But the reporting says it plainly: the last AIGC drama trade was still "the future of the AI narrative"; this time a hit variety show brought real membership and advertising revenue. The gain came from a variety show, After We Met — over 70% audience share, 200,000+ viewers in a single sponsor livestream.

· What it means That 56% was earned by a show made by people, with no AI involved. In the AIGC-drama round, the market bought the narrative, not the content.

· So what to watch To judge whether AI content can make money, don't look at the share price. Look at who is pricing quality. A narrative price is set by imagination and can move in a day; a content price is set by verified quality and takes quarters to build.
